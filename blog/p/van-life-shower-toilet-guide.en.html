Van Life Showers and Toilets: The Awkward Questions Instagram Never Answers

How do showers, toilets, laundry and hygiene actually work in a van? A practical system for water, legal disposal, odours, periods and a seven day routine.

Van life photographs contain beds, sunsets and mugs. They rarely feature a toilet cassette being carried across a car park at seven in the morning. Yet “where do you shower and go to the toilet?” usually arrives before questions about the engine.

The answer is not simply “at a petrol station”. Comfortable van life uses several options that back each other up. This guide covers showers, toilets, laundry, water, waste and intimate hygiene without pretending that a wet wipe is a complete bathroom.

The best hygiene system has three levels

  1. Daily: a basin, handwashing, teeth, face and a basic wash.
  2. Full: a van shower, campsite, swimming pool, gym or marina.
  3. Emergency: reserve water, portable toilet, towel and an offline service point.

One option will eventually fail. The pool closes, the beach shower is seasonal and the service station toilet has been “temporarily unavailable” since a previous owner.

Swimming pools, gyms, campsites, marinas and some driver facilities are often easiest. Ask whether a shower is available without an overnight booking. A beach shower usually removes salt; it is not permission for a complete wash with cosmetics. Soap does not become environmentally harmless because its label says natural.

How much water does a shower use?

Do not copy somebody else's number. Measure your shower head: collect the flow for 30 seconds and multiply by two. At 3 litres per minute, a three minute shower uses 9 litres. Drinking, handwashing, cooking and dishes sit on top of that.

The efficient method is wet, stop the water, soap, then rinse. It sounds less luxurious than a rainfall shower. The tank not running dry while shampoo is still in your hair becomes an immediately appreciated form of luxury.

Toilet options that work in daily life

Cassette toilet

It is sealed, common and supported by many service points. It needs compatible chemicals, cleaning and regular carrying. Never empty it into a storm drain, woodland or a random public toilet.

Separating toilet

It separates liquids and solids, can reduce odour with correct ventilation and may extend the interval between disposal. Both streams still need legal disposal under local rules. “Composting” does not mean a weekend has transformed the contents into garden soil.

Folding toilet or emergency bucket

Useful as a backup with strong bags and absorbent material. Waste must enter the locally appropriate stream. It is not a parcel to abandon in the first bin at a trailhead.

No toilet

This can work for short travel through areas with round the clock facilities. It is not an independent system. Traffic, illness, night and a closed station will demonstrate why.

Black water and grey water are not the same

Black water contains toilet waste. Grey water comes from sinks, showers and dishes. Both require an appropriate disposal point. Official environmental guidance explains that chemical toilet waste and grey water belong in suitable collection or treatment systems, not directly on the ground.

Read every sign at a service point. The drinking water tap should be separated from disposal equipment. Never fill a clean tank with a hose that may have rinsed a cassette. The colour of a hose is not a certificate of its personal history.

Odour is not part of the lifestyle

  • vent the toilet according to its instructions
  • empty earlier in hot weather
  • clean seals with compatible products
  • keep paper and treatment products dry
  • do not cover one problem with five fragrances
  • check whether the grey water drain is the real source

Hand hygiene matters more than shampoo fragrance

The World Health Organization recommends washing hands with soap and water before preparing food and after using the toilet. Set up a small station with a tap or pump, soap and a dedicated hand towel. Alcohol hand rub is a useful backup, but visible dirt still needs washing.

Keep drinking water equipment separate from toilet disposal equipment. Label containers and fittings. One mistake can turn a peaceful day into an extremely focused hygiene lesson.

Periods in a van without unnecessary heroics

Carry enough products for a complete cycle plus reserve, sealable waste bags, pain relief, water and a private way to wash hands. A menstrual cup requires clean hands and safe washing. Pads, tampons and wipes never belong in a cassette toilet.

For difficult days, choose a legal location with a bathroom instead of a remote viewpoint. Changing the route is not failure. It is precisely why routes are plans rather than contracts.

Laundry and drying inside a small space

A weekly laundromat is often cheaper than fighting damp. Quick drying clothes reduce volume. Never dry clothing over a hob or with ventilation blocked. Moisture moves into the mattress, walls and cupboards, then begins its own interior design project.

Use one bag for wet equipment and another for dirty clothing. The wet bag must not remain sealed for several days.

A practical seven day rhythm

  1. Daily: hands, teeth, face, intimate areas, fresh underwear and aired towels.
  2. Every two or three days: a full shower depending on activity and weather.
  3. Midweek: refill fresh water and check tank levels.
  4. Weekly: laundry and thorough cleaning of the toilet, basin and drain.
  5. Before remote travel: full water, empty waste tanks, working toilet and an offline service point.

Small van bathroom checklist

  • soap, toothbrush and toothpaste in one container
  • quick drying body towel and separate hand towel
  • paper, bags, gloves and toilet treatment
  • torch, sandals and wash bag for public showers
  • water thermometer when using heated portable systems
  • period products and necessary medication
  • separately labelled hoses for drinking water and disposal
  • legal disposal points saved offline

Frequently asked questions

Where do van lifers shower?

Inside the van, at campsites, swimming pools, gyms, marinas and selected driver facilities. A mix of options is more reliable than one membership.

Can I empty a cassette into a public toilet?

Do not assume so. Chemicals and volume may damage the system or septic tank. Use designated points and follow local instructions.

Can grey water be poured onto the ground?

No. It contains detergents, grease and food residue. Collect it and use an appropriate disposal point.

Does a solar shower work?

It can warm water in sunshine, but temperature control is poor and use depends on weather, privacy and the rules of the location.
