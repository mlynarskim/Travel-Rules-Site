Campervan Tyre Pressure and Condition: A Pre Trip Check Without Guesswork

Find the correct cold tyre pressure and check tread, damage, load, valve extensions and the spare before a campervan trip.

A campervan can have polished paint, a full water tank and a packing list ticked down to the final teaspoon, yet leave on tyres nobody has checked since the previous holiday. That is a poor trade in priorities. The tyres carry a house on wheels, brake, steer and endure long hours of heat, cold and heavy loading.

The most common mistake begins with “What pressure do you run in a van like this?” The correct answer depends on the actual vehicle, tyre size and specification, axle loads and manufacturer guidance. This guide shows where to find reliable data and how to complete a pre trip check without guessing beside the air pump.

The correct pressure is not copied from the tyre sidewall

The US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration says recommended pressure comes from the vehicle placard or owner's manual, not the maximum value moulded into the tyre sidewall. The number on the tyre is a limit for that tyre, not automatically the correct setting for your campervan.

A converted vehicle may also have information from the chassis maker and conversion manufacturer. If tyre size or wheels have changed, or real axle loads approach their limits, ask the manufacturer or a qualified tyre specialist with the load and inflation tables for the exact tyre. A forum answer from a similar looking van is not technical documentation.

Why campervan tyres deserve extra attention

  • the vehicle may stand for long periods on damp ground or in strong sun
  • it often travels close to its permitted mass with a heavily loaded rear axle
  • a high centre of gravity makes changes in handling more noticeable
  • tyres may age before the tread is worn out
  • dual rear wheels and valve extensions make checks less convenient

Pressure and load belong in the same conversation. Weigh the vehicle ready for travel and, where possible, weigh each axle. Then compare actual loads with the vehicle plate and the tyre manufacturer's data. A total mass below the overall limit does not prove that each axle is within its own limit.

How to check cold pressure step by step

  1. Find the source value. Read the placard, vehicle manual and conversion documentation. Record front, rear and spare separately.
  2. Wait before measuring. NHTSA describes a cold tyre as one that has not been driven for at least three hours. A reading after a motorway run will be higher because the tyre is warm.
  3. Use a reliable gauge. Measure every wheel with the same tool and write the numbers down rather than judging sidewall shape.
  4. Check every tyre. Include the inner tyre of a dual wheel and the spare. Replace valve caps afterwards.
  5. Adjust to documented guidance. If the correct value for the current tyres and load cannot be established, ask a competent specialist rather than inventing one.

NHTSA recommends pressure checks at least monthly and before long journeys. A campervan also deserves another check after long storage, a major temperature change, a pothole or kerb strike and before carrying a heavier load.

TPMS warns you, but it does not perform the inspection

A tyre pressure monitoring system is valuable, but its warning normally appears only after a significant pressure loss. NHTSA still recommends monthly manual checks when TPMS is fitted. The system may not monitor the spare and cannot see a crack, bulge or ageing rubber.

If a warning appears while driving, slow down without a sudden manoeuvre and stop somewhere safe. Inspect the tyre and measure its pressure. Continuing on a damaged or seriously underinflated tyre can harm its internal structure, even if air is added later.

A five minute visual inspection

AreaLook forResponse
Sidewallcracks, cuts, bulges and heavy scuffsdo not travel before professional assessment
Treaduneven wear, objects and insufficient depthmeasure and investigate the cause
Valve and extensioncracking, looseness or rubbing on the wheelrepair leakage and support
Wheel rimdistortion, corrosion and impact marksarrange a professional check
Dual wheelstrapped stones and pressure differencesinspect both tyres separately

Uneven wear may point to alignment, suspension, braking, pressure or load distribution problems. Fitting another tyre without fixing the cause can simply restart the same expensive cycle.

Tread rules are not one number for all of Europe

Minimum tread depth and seasonal requirements depend on the country and vehicle category. As one example, official UK guidance states 1.6 mm across the central three quarters and around the circumference for cars, light vans and light trailers. Different categories have different requirements there.

This is not a universal European rule. Before departure, check the official requirements for every country on the route, your vehicle category and mass, and any winter tyre rules. A legal minimum is not always a comfortable safety margin for heavy rain, snow or a long descent.

Tyre age matters even when the tread looks deep

A low mileage campervan can retain deep tread on an old tyre. Read the production date code, inspect the rubber and follow the tyre and vehicle manufacturers' guidance on inspections and replacement. Do not apply a single online age limit to every product. Storage, sunlight, loading and repair history all matter.

If the history of a used campervan is unknown, begin with a professional inspection. Ask for the tread, sidewalls, valves, internal condition, correct size, load index and wheel rims to be checked rather than relying on a quick glance.

The spare wheel and repair kit

  • check the spare against its own specified pressure, not the road wheel value
  • confirm the jack and wheel wrench suit the vehicle's mass and design
  • find the lifting points in the manual before an emergency
  • check the expiry date and limits of tyre sealant
  • save a breakdown number that accepts the campervan's weight and height

Do not change a wheel next to fast traffic, on a soft verge or unstable ground. The UK Highway Code advises changing it only when you can do so without putting yourself or others at risk. Otherwise call breakdown assistance.

Pre trip tyre checklist

  • total mass and axle loads checked
  • correct pressure confirmed from documentation
  • all tyres measured cold, including the spare
  • tread, sidewalls, valves and wheels inspected
  • no bulges, cuts or unexplained uneven wear
  • spare wheel or repair kit ready
  • breakdown cover suitable for the campervan saved on the phone

Frequently asked questions

What tyre pressure should a campervan use?

Use the placard and manuals for the specific vehicle and conversion. If tyres have changed or axle loads are high, confirm the exact tyre model, size and measured loads with a qualified specialist.

Should I inflate to the number on the tyre?

Do not treat the maximum sidewall value as the vehicle recommendation. NHTSA points to the vehicle placard and owner's manual for the correct cold pressure.

Is the TPMS warning light enough?

No. TPMS provides a warning after pressure loss, but it does not replace gauge readings or visual checks of the tread, sidewalls and valves.
