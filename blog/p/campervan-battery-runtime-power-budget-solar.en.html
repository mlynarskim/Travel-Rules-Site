How Long Will a Campervan Battery Last? Build a Power Budget Without Guessing

Calculate campervan power use in Wh, usable battery capacity, appliance runtime and a realistic daily estimate for solar charging.

Asking how long a 100 Ah campervan battery will last sounds precise, but without a list of appliances it is like asking how far a full tank will take you without naming the vehicle or road. A compressor fridge, laptop, heater controls, water pump and coffee machine have very different power habits. Inverter losses, temperature, battery age and unreliable sunshine add more variables.

The cure for guesswork is a simple energy budget in watt hours. We will move from appliance labels to daily consumption, usable battery energy and a realistic solar estimate. This is a planning guide, not a wiring manual. A competent professional should size cables, protection and any 230 V or mains installation for the specific vehicle.

Watts, watt hours and amp hours are not interchangeable

UnitWhat it describesExample
W, wattPower being drawn at a particular momentA laptop draws 60 W while charging
Wh, watt hourEnergy used over time60 W for 2 hours equals 120 Wh
Ah, amp hourBattery charge at a stated voltage100 Ah is incomplete without voltage

The foundation is energy in Wh equals power in W multiplied by time in hours. This relationship is also explained in an educational guide from the US Department of Energy. A 5 W lamp used for five hours needs 25 Wh. A 1200 W hair dryer used for ten minutes needs about 200 Wh. Brief does not always mean frugal.

Step 1: list each appliance and its daily use

Do not automatically copy the largest number printed on the casing. Record typical running power and realistic time. A fridge cycles on and off, while a laptop may draw differently during a film and a video export. A power meter or battery monitor observed over several ordinary days gives better data than an optimistic brochure.

These are sample assumptions, not promises for your equipment. Build your table from labels, manuals and measurements. Include standby loads, heater electronics and every quiet device that takes a small bite all day.

Step 2: convert the battery rating into Wh

Approximate nominal energy is voltage multiplied by capacity in Ah. A battery rated at 12.8 V and 100 Ah therefore stores about 1280 Wh nominally. That does not mean all 1280 Wh should be used routinely.

The usable portion depends on battery chemistry, its management system, temperature, age and the manufacturer's limits. Instead of applying one internet depth of discharge rule to every product, read the manual for your battery. Then keep a planning reserve for a cold night, cloudy day and the appliance that somebody definitely promised to leave at home.

Step 3: estimate days without charging

The planning formula is usable battery energy in Wh divided by daily consumption in Wh. If the manual and your chosen reserve leave 1000 Wh available and your daily use is 850 Wh, the result is about 1.18 days. That is roughly one day with a small margin, not a carefree weekend.

When an appliance runs through a 230 V inverter, include conversion losses and the inverter's own consumption. Efficiency changes with the model and load, so use manufacturer data or measurement. A large inverter rating may allow a device to start, but it does not make the battery larger.

Step 4: estimate solar input without forecasting miracles

A 300 W panel does not deliver 300 W for every daylight hour. A simple estimate is panel watts multiplied by equivalent full sun hours and whole system efficiency. Clouds, shade, dirt, panel angle, temperature and controller losses all reduce the result.

The US Department of Energy guide to solar radiation explains that available sunlight changes with location, time of day, season, weather and surroundings. Its photovoltaic technology overview describes how modules convert light into electricity. For a campervan, several days of your own readings in a similar region and season are more useful than a perfect summer advert.

Build three different power budgets

Low power day

Phone, lights, pump and fridge only. The inverter stays off, cooking uses gas and the laptop remains closed. This is your useful baseline for shade or poor weather.

Remote work day

Add a laptop, router and extra charging. Do not count only the charger label. Measure a whole day including calls, uploads and that late evening cloud restore nobody planned.

Comfort day

A coffee machine, hair dryer, induction hob or other high power appliance can consume a large share quickly. Check energy in Wh, inverter peak and continuous power, the battery's permitted current and the system protection.

Safety that no spreadsheet can calculate

  • never fit a larger fuse merely to stop it tripping
  • do not use a damaged or unknown cable and adapter for campsite power
  • do not route damaged leads under rugs or across sharp edges
  • have cable sizes, protection and mains work specified by a competent professional
  • check regularly for loose connections, heat damage and the smell of melting plastic

The US National Park Service recommends keeping the RV shore power cord in good condition and using a wire gauge suitable for the electrical load. It also advises checking 12 V connections. If a plug is scorched, tape and optimism are not a repair plan.

Reduce consumption without living by candlelight

  1. Start with long running loads such as the fridge, router and standby electronics.
  2. Charge while driving only when the installation is designed correctly for it.
  3. Do not run the mains inverter for jobs that can be handled efficiently at 12 V.
  4. Move work and charging into the best solar production hours.
  5. Compare the plan with battery monitor data for several days and adjust it.

Frequently asked questions

How long will a 100 Ah campervan battery last?

It depends on voltage, chemistry, the permitted discharge range and daily energy use. Convert capacity to Wh, apply the manufacturer's limits and divide usable energy by your measured daily consumption.

Will a 200 W solar panel charge a 100 Ah battery?

It can provide charging energy, but the time depends on how much energy is missing, sunlight, shade, temperature and system losses. Panel nameplate power alone cannot give a reliable charging time.

What uses the most electricity in a campervan?

High power appliances and devices that run for many hours dominate the budget. Compare watts multiplied by hours, not watts alone.
