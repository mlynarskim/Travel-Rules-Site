Campervan Condensation and Mould: How to Keep Windows, Walls and Mattresses Dry

A practical campervan moisture plan covering condensation diagnosis, ventilation, heating, mattress airflow and safe mould cleanup.

A campervan morning can begin with what looks like a private weather system: fogged windows, droplets on the frames and a damp patch under the mattress. Occasional condensation is common. Repeated campervan condensation is a warning that moisture is entering faster than the vehicle can remove it. Left alone, it can damage plywood, upholstery, insulation and electrics, while mould can affect health.

The answer is not one moisture absorber parked beside your shoes. You need a small operating system: reduce water vapour, ventilate deliberately, heat steadily, keep air moving behind furniture and deal with wet materials quickly. This guide works for converted vans, motorhomes and caravans.

Where campervan moisture comes from

Breathing, cooking, showering, wet clothing and some fuel burning appliances all release water vapour. When warm humid air meets a cold window, metal rib or poorly insulated panel, it cools and releases liquid water. That is why the problem is often worst overnight, in winter and whenever indoor and outdoor temperatures are far apart.

The US Environmental Protection Agency recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60 percent and ideally between 30 and 50 percent. In a tiny vehicle, use that as a practical reference rather than a magic number. Persistent droplets, musty smells and fabrics that never dry matter even if the display looks reassuring. Read the official EPA guide to moisture and mould.

Condensation or a leak: diagnose before buying a dehumidifier

ClueMore like condensationMore like a leak
TimingAfter sleeping, cooking or showeringAfter rain, washing or driving through water
LocationWindows, metal, corners, mattress baseOne point near a window, roof fitting or service opening
PatternSeveral cold surfaces at onceA stain, swollen board or wet trail
ResponseImproves with ventilation and heatReturns even when the interior air is dry

Dry everything, mark the edge of the stain with a pencil and inspect it after the next rain. Check window seals, roof vents, aerials, awning fixings, water fillers and cable entries. If one area repeatedly gets wet, fix the opening first. Drying without repairing a leak is simply mopping with the tap still running.

Start with a hygrometer and a map of cold spots

A small temperature and humidity meter costs less than replacing a mattress. Record conditions after waking, before bed, after cooking and during rain. Keep the sensor away from a heater outlet and the cold glass. Feel or measure rear doors, cab over corners, storage spaces, furniture backs and areas around water tanks.

For one week, save the morning reading, weather, number of occupants, cooking and location of droplets in the Travel Rules app. A pattern will usually reveal whether the trigger is a sealed night, soaked outdoor kit or one suspicious seal.

Ventilation and heating must work as a pair

Heating alone lets air hold more vapour for a while, but does not remove it from the vehicle. Throwing one window wide open for hours can chill every surface. A steady low airflow with a stable safe temperature is usually more effective.

  1. Keep manufacturer fitted permanent ventilation openings clear.
  2. While cooking, run the extractor or crack a window opposite it to create a path.
  3. After a shower, close the bathroom door, exhaust steam outside and squeegee the walls.
  4. In the morning, air the whole vehicle briefly and wipe windows with an absorbent cloth.
  5. Never block safety vents because they feel draughty.

The mattress: invisible until the first black dot

Your body warms the mattress while the platform below stays cold. Vapour moves down and condenses underneath. Create an air gap with slats, a spacer mat or a ventilated bed board. Do not wedge bedding tightly between the mattress and an outside wall. Lift the mattress every few days and inspect its underside, especially around corners.

Do not dry wet jackets, towels and boots over the bed. Use a wet zone near an extractor or keep them contained until they can dry outdoors. Fold the duvet back in the morning so overnight moisture can escape instead of being pressed into the mattress.

Cook without turning the van into a steam room

  • Use lids and match the pan to the burner.
  • Drain boiling water only with an open vent or running extractor.
  • Avoid long cooking sessions without air exchange.
  • Use fuel burning ovens and hobs only as their manufacturer instructs.
  • Never use a cooking appliance to heat the living space.

Carbon monoxide has no colour or smell. The US Consumer Product Safety Commission warns against using portable fuel burning camping equipment inside vehicles unless it is specifically designed and instructed for enclosed use. Fit and test an appropriate CO alarm, keep ventilation clear and follow the heater manual. See the official CPSC camping equipment guidance.

Does a campervan dehumidifier help

A chemical absorber needs no electricity but has limited capacity and cannot replace ventilation. A small thermoelectric unit is quiet but often weak in cold conditions. A compressor model extracts more water, yet needs suitable temperatures, storage and electricity. A desiccant machine performs better in the cold but can consume substantial power.

Match the tool to your temperature and genuine spare energy. If its tank fills every day, treat that as evidence, not victory. Find out why so much water is entering or being generated.

What to do when mould has appeared

Photograph the area, locate the moisture and stop it first. The CDC advises at least an N95 respirator, gloves and goggles for mould cleanup. People with asthma, chronic lung disease, allergies or immune suppression should not do the work themselves. Follow the official CDC mould cleanup guidance.

Clean a small hard surface with water and detergent, then dry it completely. Never mix cleaning products, especially bleach and ammonia. A porous mattress, wallboard or insulation with established growth may need replacement. Call a qualified professional when growth is extensive, keeps returning, has entered a wall or leaves a persistent musty smell.

The whole plan in one sentence

Measure humidity, identify the source, exhaust vapour, heat steadily, move air under the mattress and deal with wet areas on the same day. A campervan will never be a dry laboratory, but it should not start a mushroom farm behind the wardrobe.
