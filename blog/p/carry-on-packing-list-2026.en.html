Carry On Packing List 2026: What You Can Bring on a Plane

A complete carry on packing list for 2026 covering liquids, toiletries, power banks, medication, electronics, food, documents and prohibited items.

A well packed carry on saves money, time, and stress. A badly packed one can lead to a gate fee, discarded toiletries at security, or a power bank being found after the suitcase has already been checked. In 2026 the key principle is simple: there is no single worldwide size and weight allowance, but there are widely used safety rules for liquids, batteries, and dangerous items.

This guide combines a practical carry on packing list with current aviation guidance. Always run one final check on the airline, departure airport, transit airport, and destination websites because a carrier or country may apply stricter requirements.

Carry on bag, personal item, and checked baggage

Before packing, confirm exactly what the fare includes. A personal item is usually a small backpack or handbag that fits under the seat. A carry on suitcase normally goes in the overhead locker. Checked baggage travels in the hold. Many basic fares include only one personal item.

Do not assume the allowance from your previous flight still applies. Limits can depend on the airline, fare family, cabin, route, and aircraft. Check height, width, depth, weight, the number of pieces, and whether wheels and handles count. Save a screenshot of the rules attached to your booking.

Carry on liquid rules in 2026

At European Union airports, the standard rule allows liquids through security in individual containers no larger than 100 ml. The containers go into one transparent resealable bag with a capacity of no more than one liter. The container size matters, not the amount left inside. A 200 ml bottle with a small amount of shampoo may be rejected.

Liquids also include gels, pastes, creams, aerosols, and mixtures. Toothpaste, perfume, mascara, lotion, peanut butter, and yogurt can all fall into this category. Essential medicine, baby food, and special dietary products needed for the journey can qualify for exceptions and additional screening.

  • Transfer toiletries into labeled containers of 100 ml or less.
  • Keep the liquid bag easy to remove.
  • Protect caps against leaks.
  • Check the transit airport if you pass through security again.
  • Keep duty free liquids sealed with the receipt in the security bag.

Power banks, batteries, and electronics

Power banks and spare lithium batteries belong in the cabin, not checked baggage. IATA guidance says terminals should be protected from short circuits and the watt hour rating should be checked. Batteries up to 100 Wh are generally accepted in carry on baggage. Larger batteries may need airline approval, and an airline can impose stricter conditions.

IATA guidance for 2026 also explains that airlines may limit the number of power banks, prohibit charging them from aircraft power, and require them to remain visible and accessible. Check the actual carrier. If a cabin bag is taken at the gate and placed in the hold, remove power banks, spare batteries, vapes, and valuable electronics first.

  • Keep phones, laptops, cameras, and tablets in the cabin when possible.
  • Never travel with a swollen, damaged, or recalled battery.
  • Protect spare battery terminals with a case or tape.
  • Smart luggage should have a removable battery.
  • Confirm drones and larger camera batteries with the airline.

Medication and medical equipment

Pack medication for the entire trip plus a reasonable reserve in hand luggage. Keep it in original labeled packaging when possible. For prescriptions, needles, liquid medication over 100 ml, medical devices, or controlled substances, carry a prescription or medical letter, ideally with an English version.

Permission to carry an item on the aircraft does not automatically mean it can be imported into the destination. Check the destination and transit country rules. Pack temperature sensitive medicine according to the manufacturer guidance and declare it during screening.

Food and drinks

Solid snacks are often accepted in the cabin, while sauces, soup, yogurt, and creamy foods can count as liquids. An empty water bottle can normally pass through security and be filled afterward. Customs restrictions on arrival are a separate issue. Meat, dairy, fruit, seeds, and plants may need declaration or may be prohibited.

If you are unsure, consume the product before landing or declare it. Concealing food can lead to a penalty even when the item was legal during the flight.

Sharp and prohibited items

Knives, larger scissors, tools, some razor types, sports equipment, and objects that could be used as weapons should not be placed in carry on baggage. Exact thresholds vary by country and airport. Explosives, fireworks, and many flammable products are prohibited in both cabin and checked baggage.

Do not judge an item only by common sense. Camping stoves, defensive sprays, gas cartridges, lighters, multitools, and electric bicycle batteries have detailed rules. Check the official prohibited item list before leaving for the airport.

Documents and valuables that should stay with you

  • passport, identity card, and required travel authorization
  • phone, wallet, cards, and cash
  • medication and medical documents
  • home and vehicle keys
  • laptop, camera, and data drives
  • jewelry and irreplaceable personal items
  • one spare outfit
  • offline accommodation, ticket, and insurance details

A checked bag can be delayed. Pack the cabin bag so you can manage the first 24 hours without your suitcase.

Complete carry on packing list

Documents

  • passport or identity card
  • visa or authorization when required
  • boarding pass
  • insurance and emergency contacts
  • bookings available offline

Electronics

  • phone and cable
  • plug adapter
  • airline compliant power bank
  • headphones
  • laptop or reader when needed

Comfort

  • empty water bottle
  • light sweater or scarf
  • eye mask and earplugs
  • tissues
  • snack

24 hour emergency kit

  • medication
  • underwear and a shirt
  • toothbrush
  • small deodorant that follows the liquid rule
  • essential hygiene products

Pack the bag in layers

Place items needed only after arrival at the bottom. Put clothes and organizers in the middle. Keep electronics in a protected section near your back. Put liquids, documents, medication, and security items at the top. Use an outer pocket for the empty bottle, tissues, and a charging cable.

Weigh the bag at home and measure it after packing. A soft backpack can exceed the limit when every pocket is full. Leave a little room for food, a removed clothing layer, or a small purchase.

Common carry on mistakes

  • Checking dimensions but ignoring weight.
  • Putting a power bank in checked baggage.
  • Using a large container with only a little product inside.
  • Packing medication and documents in the checked suitcase.
  • Burying everything that must be removed at security.
  • Assuming departure airport rules are identical at the connection.
  • Not checking the fare before arriving at the airport.

Frequently asked carry on questions

Can deodorant go in carry on baggage?

Yes, but liquid, gel, and aerosol deodorant is subject to liquid limits. A solid stick normally does not need to go in the liquid bag. Verify local rules.

Can you bring a razor on a plane?

Disposable razors with enclosed blades are commonly accepted, while loose blades and straight razors are not. Rules vary by security authority.

Can you use a power bank during a flight?

Some airlines limit or prohibit power bank use and charging. Check the carrier policy and follow crew instructions.

Can an empty bottle go through security?

Usually yes. It must be empty, and you can fill it after security when the airport has a refill point.

Final check before leaving home

  • Size, weight, and number of pieces checked in the booking
  • Liquids in containers up to 100 ml inside the one liter bag
  • Power banks and spare batteries in the cabin
  • Medication, documents, and valuables accessible
  • No sharp or prohibited items
  • Bag arranged for fast screening
  • Essential information saved offline

Official sources: European Commission air traveler security guidance, IATA passenger baggage rules, and IATA lithium battery guidance.
