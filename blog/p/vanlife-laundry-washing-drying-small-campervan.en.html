Campervan Laundry Without a Utility Room: Wash, Dry and Avoid Damp

A practical vanlife laundry system covering dirty storage, laundromats, hand washing, wet weather drying and a compact travel wardrobe.

Campervan laundry begins innocently. One T shirt lands on the passenger seat, a towel hangs from the door and socks enter a carrier bag labelled “later”. Three days later the van has its own climate and locating clean underwear feels like archaeology. The real problem is not the lack of a washing machine. It is the lack of a simple system from taking clothes off to returning them dry to the cupboard.

A useful vanlife laundry routine should save water, time and space. It must also work in rain, in a city, at a campsite and when the nearest laundromat has three stars, two of which may belong to the owner. Here is a practical system for solo travellers, couples and families.

Create three zones instead of one mysterious bag

The simplest split is clean, wearable again and genuinely dirty. A jumper worn for an hour does not need to live beside wet socks. Hang once more clothes from one dedicated hook or place them in a ventilated basket. Keep dry dirty laundry in a removable breathable bag. Wet items should dry first or stay in a separate container that you open at the next opportunity.

  • Clean: folded vertically or separated in organisers.
  • Wear again: one shelf, hook or mesh pocket.
  • Dirty and dry: a light bag that lifts out as one load.
  • Wet: a separate container only until drying or washing.

Do not seal a wet towel inside the laundry bag for days. The smell is the least interesting consequence. Moisture spreads into other fabrics and the surrounding storage.

A mixed system is usually best. Hand wash small essentials before they take control of the wardrobe, then take a proper load to a machine every few days. Carrying two weeks of damp laundry to save one wash is not a bargain.

How to choose a laundromat on the road

Check recent photos, opening hours, payment method and whether dryers are available. Some machines accept coins only, while others need a card or app. Look at parking access too, especially height restrictions and the length of your van.

The best laundry day combines errands such as groceries, water, showers, remote work or a walk around town. Add it as a normal route item in the Travel Planner instead of treating it as three lost hours. Leave time for a busy machine and a dryer programme that lasts longer than the screen promised.

Hand washing without emptying the water tank

  1. Choose a small load with similar colours and care needs.
  2. Use a basin, folding bucket or sealed wash bag.
  3. Measure a small amount of detergent according to its instructions. More foam means more rinsing.
  4. Soak only when the fabric needs it.
  5. Gently work the clothes instead of scrubbing everything like a renovation rug.
  6. Rinse in batches and press water out without twisting delicate items.
  7. Roll each item in a dry towel and press to remove more water before drying.

Do not empty detergent water randomly onto the ground. Use a designated drain or sanitary facility and follow site rules. A biodegradable label does not automatically turn a hedge into a wastewater point.

Drying outside without creating a residential exhibition

At a campsite or place where it is permitted, use a short line between your own van fittings or a folding rack. Do not attach lines to protected trees, somebody else's infrastructure or anything that could be damaged. Keep clothes clear of paths and make sure they do not drip onto neighbours.

  • shake items before hanging so they dry faster and crease less
  • give thick fabric air on both sides
  • turn pockets out
  • use pegs even when the wind seems polite
  • bring everything in before evening damp arrives

What if it rains for three days?

Indoor drying requires airflow and moisture control. Hang a small amount at a time, use the ventilation designed for the vehicle and maintain a steady temperature. Never cover fixed vents, heaters or exits. Wet fabric should not touch the mattress, walls or wooden cabinets.

If water streams down the windows, the van smells musty or the mattress base is damp in the morning, stop drying indoors and use a tumble dryer. Read the related guide to campervan condensation and mould. One paid drying cycle is normally cheaper than replacing a mattress.

Build a wardrobe that is easier to wash

The easiest laundry system starts with clothes that combine well, dry quickly and share similar care instructions. Choose two or three basic colour groups. Limit pieces that demand a separate programme, ironing or a whole afternoon to dry.

  • pack layers instead of many bulky outfits
  • choose fabrics suited to your skin and climate, not merely labelled technical
  • carry two quick drying towels so one can dry properly
  • keep one clean emergency outfit in a separate bag
  • repair tiny tears before a washing machine turns them into windows

Solo, couple and family laundry

Solo

Tiny machine loads become expensive. Pick a regular bigger wash day and hand wash only essentials between visits. Do not expand the wardrobe merely to fill a drum.

Couple

One shared bag works when both people use the same rules. Decide who checks pockets, chooses programmes and remembers collection. “I thought that tissue was yours” will not restore the black T shirts.

Family

Keep a small urgent bag for stained items and one spare outfit outside the normal rotation. Wash smaller loads more often. Plan a playground or walk near the laundromat rather than delivering a two hour lecture on patience.

A simple laundry day routine

  1. Sort by colour, programme and drying method the evening before.
  2. Check pockets, stains and care labels in the morning.
  3. Pack detergent, payment, bags and one clean cloth.
  4. Never return damp clothes to a closed cupboard.
  5. Put clean clothes away immediately and dry the laundry bag.
  6. Record the cost and approximate next wash day.

Frequently asked questions

How often should you do laundry in vanlife?

Often enough that the bag stays inside its allocated space and never stores moisture. A solo traveller may run a larger weekly load, while a family will wash more often. The real limits are clothing volume and drying capacity.

How do you dry clothes in a campervan in winter?

Use small batches, steady ventilation and heat, without covering vents. When humidity rises or clothes refuse to dry, use a tumble dryer.

Is a mini washing machine worth it in a van?

Only after counting storage, water, electricity, drainage and drying time. For many travellers, a laundromat every few days consumes less space and attention than a permanent appliance.
