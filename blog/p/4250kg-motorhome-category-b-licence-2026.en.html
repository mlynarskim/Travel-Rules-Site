Can You Drive a 4.25 Tonne Motorhome on a Car Licence? The 2026 Headlines Are Ahead of the Law

Can a 4,250 kg motorhome be driven on a category B car licence in 2026? Current limits, exceptions, category C1 and future EU code 96.02 explained.

Short answer on 24 August 2026: a standard category B car licence does not automatically let you drive a conventional motorhome with a maximum authorised mass of 4,250 kg. The European Union has adopted rules enabling a new entitlement after training or testing, but the main provisions are due to apply nationally from 26 November 2029. Buying a heavy motorhome in 2026 on the strength of a headline is an expensive form of optimism.

The question matters because a 3.5 tonne motorhome can have painfully little payload. Four people, water, bikes, gas and accessories turn a holiday into advanced subtraction. A 4.25 tonne limit is useful, but a future rule is not yet an entitlement printed on your licence.

The decisive figure is MAM, maximum authorised mass, not unladen weight and not what the vehicle happens to weigh today. Removing the plates may save a kilogram. It does not amend the registration document.

What category B covers today

Across current national systems based on the existing EU categories, B normally covers a motor vehicle up to 3.5 tonnes MAM and no more than eight passenger seats in addition to the driver. A self propelled motorhome rated at 3,850 or 4,250 kg does not become a category B vehicle because it carries a bed instead of freight.

National exceptions for alternatively fuelled vehicles may exist. Poland, for example, allows a category B holder of at least two years to drive an alternatively fuelled vehicle up to 4.25 tonnes when the excess above 3.5 tonnes results from the propulsion system and is recorded in the registration document. That is a technical exception, not permission for every heavy motorhome. Confirm it for the exact vehicle and issuing country.

C1, C, B96 and BE solve different problems

  • C1: normally covers motor vehicles above 3.5 tonnes and up to 7.5 tonnes. It is the usual current answer for a heavier motorhome.
  • C: is the broader heavy vehicle category above 3.5 tonnes.
  • B96: concerns a category B towing vehicle and trailer where the combination exceeds 3.5 tonnes but does not exceed 4.25 tonnes. It does not raise the permitted MAM of the motorhome itself.
  • BE: expands trailer capability. It also does not turn a 4.25 tonne motorhome into a category B vehicle.

The classic mistake is “I have B96, so I can drive a 4.25 tonne motorhome.” B96 deals with weight behind a category B towing vehicle. It does not put the motorhome on a paper diet.

What the European Union actually adopted

Directive EU 2025/2205 creates an entitlement linked to category B for a motor caravan above 3,500 kg and up to 4,250 kg. With a trailer, the combination may reach 5,000 kg. The entitlement will be shown with Union code 96.02.

It is not an automatic extension of every B licence. A Member State must require targeted training, a practical test or both. The directive sets a minimum of seven hours when training is used, covering heavy vehicle dynamics, braking, manoeuvres, loading and load security.

When will the new motorhome licence rule apply

The directive entered into force on 25 November 2025, but that phrase is routinely misread. Member States must adopt the main implementing measures by 26 November 2028 and apply them from 26 November 2029. They still need national procedures for training or testing, issuing the entitlement and adding code 96.02.

The European Commission’s own summary says new rights for heavy motor caravans will follow targeted training or testing decided by the Member State. If a rental company says “the EU already allows it”, ask for the current national provision and the code on your licence.

Rules are not identical in every country yet

The directive sets the destination, but national law, the issuing authority and recognition rules matter until implementation. Spain’s DGT still states that a motorhome above 3,500 kg requires the corresponding higher category. Poland likewise retains the general 3.5 tonne category B limit, with its narrow alternative fuel exception. A blog post from another country cannot add a category to your licence.

Why 4,250 kg matters so much

A motorhome plated at 3,500 kg may have a very small usable payload. Start with the ready to travel vehicle, then add passengers, water, fuel, gas, bikes, awning, cables and luggage. Manufacturer mass figures do not always include every option fitted later.

Higher MAM provides breathing room but also changes obligations. Speed limits, toll systems, heavy vehicle restrictions, tyre rules and access rules may differ. In Poland, for example, a motorhome above 3.5 tonnes uses e TOLL on the charged network. Read our current guide to Poland e TOLL for motorhomes and caravans.

What to check before buying or renting

  1. Read MAM from the registration document, not the advert.
  2. Check your licence category, codes and first issue date.
  3. Confirm whether a stated alternative fuel exception applies to this vehicle.
  4. Compare ready to travel weight with MAM and axle limits.
  5. Weigh the vehicle with normal passengers, water and equipment.
  6. Check toll, speed and access rules for every country on the route.
  7. Require the rental company to state the licence category in writing.

Three scenarios that catch drivers

The motorhome is plated at 3,500 kg but weighs 3,650 kg

The licence matches the documented MAM, but the vehicle is overloaded. That is a separate offence and a genuine tyre, axle and braking risk.

The motorhome is plated at 4,250 kg but weighs only 3,400 kg today

An ordinary B licence still does not suffice. Licence categories use MAM, not today’s scale ticket.

I have B96 and a 4,250 kg motorhome with no trailer

B96 does not help. It applies to a trailer combination behind a category B vehicle, not to a heavy self propelled vehicle.

Frequently asked questions

Can everyone drive a 4,250 kg motorhome on B from 2026

No. The EU directive exists, but the main national measures are due to apply from 26 November 2029.

Will the new entitlement be automatic

No. The directive provides for additional training, a practical test or both, followed by code 96.02.

Does B96 cover a 4.25 tonne motorhome

No. B96 concerns a vehicle and trailer combination, not the motorhome itself.

What licence is needed for a 3,850 kg motorhome today

Usually C1 or an appropriate higher category, unless a precisely documented national alternative fuel exception applies. Verify before driving.
