Is Van Life Still Cheap? The Real Cost of Living in a Van in Europe in 2026

Van life is often sold as living without rent. That is true in the same way a free app can contain purchases: the headline is technically correct, but you should read the rest of the screen. You may stop paying a landlord, yet the fuel station, garage, motorway and laundromat become remarkably interested in your wallet.

This guide answers how much van life costs in Europe in 2026 without inventing one magical average. It gives you three transparent scenarios, formulas for your own route and the expenses people forget. Every figure is a planning example, not a promise that every country will charge the same.

The most useful number is not your monthly total

Calculate the cost of one stationary day and the cost of driving 100 km. Those figures reveal whether movement or daily life controls the budget.

Fuel cost per 100 km equals consumption multiplied by the price per litre. A van using 9 litres at €1.70 costs €15.30 per 100 km. Driving 3000 km then costs €459. Removing 1000 km from the route saves more than heroic attempts to save twenty cents on pasta.

Fuel prices change weekly and vary across countries. Check the European Commission's Weekly Oil Bulletin instead of trusting a screenshot from six months ago.

The four envelopes of an honest budget

  1. Vehicle: finance or depreciation, insurance, tax, inspection and servicing.
  2. Movement: fuel, tolls, vignettes, ferries, parking and urban zones.
  3. Life: food, mobile data, showers, laundry, gas, water and campsites.
  4. Failure: tyres, brakes, heating, batteries and accommodation during repairs.

Keep the repair fund separate from normal spending. If nothing breaks during a good month, the balance does not become a restaurant bonus. It waits for the month when a mechanic begins a sentence with “unfortunately”.

The cost of a van before the first kilometre

The purchase price is only the opening line. Add a pre purchase inspection, registration, first service, tyres, rust repair, equipment, gas installation, electrics and anything required by the insurer. Keep cash after buying. A van purchased with every available euro is an expensive waiting room for the first failure.

For a self build, separate materials from tools. Screws, cable, fuses, adhesives and delivery charges can form a budget monster made entirely from tiny receipts.

Road charges do not end with a vignette

Europe uses systems based on time, distance, vehicle class, weight, height and emissions. The same van may enter another price category after crossing a weight or axle threshold. The European Commission explains the common framework for road tolls and vignettes, while countries set the actual schemes.

Before leaving, calculate motorways, tunnels, bridges, ferries and city access. The shortest route is not always cheapest. The scenic detour is not automatically a saving if it burns more fuel and consumes another day.

Food: a small kitchen does not require a large bill

The number of restaurant meals matters more than the brand of oats. Set a weekly eating out limit, plan three days at a time and buy packs you will finish. A large pack is cheap only if half of it does not become waste.

If you travel without active cooling, use the practical guide to van cooking without a fridge. It contains complete meals and a shopping list rather than instructions to “use whatever you have”.

Parking: a free night can still cost you

Free overnight parking works when it is legal, safe and does not require a 60 km detour. Sometimes a €25 campsite includes water, a shower, electricity, laundry access and toilet disposal. That single payment replaces several separate expenses. Compare the bundle, not just the pitch price.

The expenses most budgets forget

  • laundry and drying during wet weather
  • gas bottles and country specific adapters
  • parking beside a laundromat, city centre or attraction
  • backup internet and roaming outside the EU
  • shipping a part to a remote garage
  • a hotel during a major repair
  • health care and prescriptions
  • replacing possessions damaged by moisture
  • fines and administration fees
  • vehicle and conversion depreciation

The article about hidden van life costs explores the surprises. This guide adds a monthly model and formulas.

How to spend less without ruining the trip

  1. Stay longer in each region.
  2. Fill up before an expensive stretch, without carrying illegal fuel containers.
  3. Combine a campsite visit with laundry, water, showers and servicing.
  4. Cook portions without leftovers and shop for three days.
  5. Service brakes, tyres and heating before they fail.
  6. Confirm vehicle weight and class before buying a vignette.
  7. Plan a no driving day after every long stretch.

The weekly sheet worth keeping

Record date, country, kilometres, litres, parking, food, tolls, repairs and other spending. After four weeks calculate cost per day and cost per kilometre. Do not judge the budget from one expensive ferry week or one unusually cheap week in a free location.

Frequently asked questions

Is van life cheaper than renting?

It can be with slow travel, a paid off vehicle and a proper repair reserve. Expensive finance, frequent driving and regular campsites can make it cost more.

How much money do you need to start?

Beyond the vehicle, budget for preparation, the first service, equipment, insurance and a separate emergency reserve. Do not spend every available euro on the purchase.

Where can van lifers save the most?

On mileage, restaurant meals and unplanned stops. Slower travel often reduces fuel, tolls and panic purchases at the same time.

Are free overnight stops really free?

Not always. Include the fuel to reach them, missing services, a separate shower and your time. A paid service area can be cheaper overall.
