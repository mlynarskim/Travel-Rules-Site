Campervan Payload Without Guesswork: How to Weigh the Vehicle, Axles and Every Bag

A practical guide to campervan payload: plated limits, real travel weight, axle loads, water, passengers and weighbridge checks.

A campervan looks light enough until you add an awning, bike rack, two bikes, full water, gas, food, a dog and the “just in case” toolbox nobody has opened since 2022. Payload is not a brochure number. It is the difference between the vehicle's plated limit and its real weight on departure day.

The essential step is to weigh the travel ready vehicle and each axle. A total alone is not enough because a camper can sit below its overall limit while overloading the rear axle. This guide explains how to read the plate, prepare a meaningful weighbridge visit and recover useful margin without throwing out your toothbrush.

Four weights that are easy to confuse

TermMeaningWhere to find it
Actual weightWhat the vehicle weighs now with people and cargoWeighbridge result
Maximum authorised massMaximum safe vehicle weight including its loadPlate, documents and manual
Maximum axle loadSeparate limit for front and rear axleVehicle plate
Gross train weightVehicle, trailer and both loads togetherPlate if towing is approved

Official GOV.UK guidance defines maximum authorised mass as the weight of a vehicle or trailer including the maximum load it can safely carry. Documents may use MAM, GVW or permissible maximum weight. Always use the plate and documents for your exact vehicle plus the rules of its registration country. See the official vehicle weights explanation.

The payload formula is simple, the inputs are not

Remaining payload equals plated maximum mass minus actual travel weight. If the plate says 3500 kg and the ready camper weighs 3380 kg, 120 kg remains. That margin must cover everything not present at the weighbridge, including another passenger, shopping, extra water, fuel, sports equipment or trailer nose weight.

Do not subtract a catalogue empty weight from the limit and call the result payload. Accessories may have been added later, while the declared mass may assume a different amount of fluids, equipment or driver allowance from the one you actually travel with.

What belongs in the camper during weighing

  • driver and ideally every passenger, or their accurately recorded mass
  • normal fuel, AdBlue and gas levels
  • the water amount planned for departure
  • food, clothing, bedding and cookware
  • bikes, scooter, chairs, table, grill and sports gear
  • tools, cables, levelling blocks, spare fluids and parts
  • pets, carriers and their supplies
  • every permanent accessory fitted after purchase

The best configuration is “we leave tomorrow”. An empty fridge and absent passenger produce a prettier ticket, but not the answer you need.

How to weigh a campervan step by step

  1. Find a properly operated vehicle weighbridge that can report total and individual axle weights.
  2. Read the vehicle plate and note total, axle and any train limits.
  3. Load the camper exactly as for travel.
  4. Place every wheel on the scale and record the total.
  5. Measure front and rear axle loads as directed by the operator.
  6. Compare every result with its own limit, not only the sum.
  7. Record the date, water, fuel, people and accessories included.

The UK Driver and Vehicle Standards Agency advises knowing vehicle and load weights, establishing individual axle loads where possible and checking gross weight before setting out. Its official overloading guidance is written for heavy goods vehicles, but the method of checking plates, gross mass and axle limits is useful for motorhomes too.

Why a legal looking total can hide an overloaded axle

Imagine a camper with a 3500 kg total limit, 1850 kg front axle limit and 2000 kg rear limit. The scale shows 3450 kg, apparently leaving 50 kg. But if the front is 1500 kg and the rear 1950 kg, two bikes and a box mounted far behind the rear axle may overload the back even when the overall total remains close to its limit.

Weight behind an axle acts through leverage. It can add more load to the rear axle than the item's own mass while unloading the front. Steering feel and stability may change. Keep heavy equipment low, close to the vehicle centre and between the axles where possible.

Small looking items that consume real payload

ItemCountCommon mistake
WaterAbout 1 kg per litre plus tank hardwareWeighing with an empty tank
Gas cylindersCylinder, gas and restraintCounting only the gas
BikesBikes, rack, locks and batteriesIgnoring the rack
AwningThe complete fitted assemblyAssuming it was in the base mass
PeopleActual bodies and carried itemsUsing one brochure allowance

One hundred litres of water is roughly one hundred kilograms before the tank itself. If reliable water is available at the destination, driving it across half of Europe may make little sense. Do not reduce it so far that the pump runs dry or the journey depends on the first tap being open.

The load must also stay in place

Being within weight limits does not help if a toolbox launches forward under braking. Put heavy items low and secure them to points designed for load restraint. Latch cupboards, lock drawers and remove loose objects from the cab over bed.

EU roadworthiness rules say cargo distribution must respect maximum axle loads and that securing should prevent sliding, tipping and overturning. The principles appear in Annex III of the directive on EUR Lex.

How to recover payload without ruining the trip

  1. Remove duplicate items that performed no useful job on the last two trips.
  2. Weigh heavy accessories individually instead of guessing.
  3. Move weight from behind the rear axle toward the vehicle centre.
  4. Match water volume to genuine availability on the route.
  5. Check whether an unused rack or additional battery can be safely removed.
  6. Never treat higher tyre pressure as a cure for exceeding a plated limit.

If there is no safe margin after a sensible clear out, you may need a legally approved uprating or a different vehicle. A new plate does not automatically make axles, tyres, brakes, licence entitlements and toll categories suitable. Also read the guide to 4.25 tonne motorhomes and category B licences.

Keep the result with the vehicle records

Photograph the plate and save the weighbridge ticket. In the Travel Rules app, record the total, both axle loads, tank configuration and date. Schedule another weighing after adding an awning, solar system, rack, battery or conversion furniture.

Before a first trip, the ebook on preparing a campervan for its first journey can help with the rest of the vehicle checks. A packing list tells you what to bring. A scale tells you how much of it the vehicle can safely carry.

Departure checklist

  • Total mass below the plated limit.
  • Front and rear axle below their separate limits.
  • People, water, fuel, gas and complete equipment included.
  • Heavy items low and close to the vehicle centre.
  • Cupboards, bikes, cylinders and boxes properly secured.
  • A sensible margin rather than exactly zero kilograms.

Legal requirements, enforcement tolerances and driving licence rules vary by country. Check the vehicle documents and the rules for its place of registration and travel. Sources checked on 18 September 2026.
