Europe’s Low Emission Zones by Motorhome: Stickers, Registrations and 2026 Traps

Updated 28 August 2026. A motorhome can cross a thousand kilometres of motorway perfectly legally and still get into trouble on the last three streets before a campsite. The culprit is often a low emission zone, a foreign plate registration or a windscreen sticker that cannot be summoned from a petrol station at 10:40 pm.

Europe does not have one universal environmental badge. A French Crit’Air sticker does not replace Germany’s Umweltplakette, and registering for Brussels does not automatically clear Antwerp or Barcelona. The practical system below keeps route planning manageable without turning your windscreen into a sticker album.

Check the city, not merely the country

National law may create the framework, but regions and cities define boundaries, hours, vehicle classes and exemptions. A camper can be registered as M1, a special purpose motor caravan, N1 or a heavier category. Cameras see a plate; the rulebook may also care about the vehicle category, Euro standard, fuel, first registration date and authorised mass.

Before planning, copy those details from the registration certificate and keep a legible scan. It is far easier to solve the paperwork at home than to enlarge field V.9 on a phone while the campsite reception closes.

France: order Crit’Air before departure

France uses the Crit’Air certificate to classify vehicles by emissions. The official service states that it is required in low emission mobility zones and during differentiated traffic restrictions imposed in pollution episodes. Owners of foreign registered vehicles can order online by entering the vehicle details and uploading the registration document. Use only the official Crit’Air website; resellers often add an impressive fee without adding an impressive sticker.

The badge alone does not guarantee entry. Each metropolitan zone decides which classes are restricted and when. The official Greater Paris page explains its current perimeter and the treatment of Crit’Air 3 vehicles. Check the current Greater Paris ZFE rules, hours and exemptions immediately before travel.

Germany: the green sticker still matters

Vehicles entering an active German Umweltzone need the correct windscreen sticker. Germany’s Federal Environment Agency says only the green sticker has been accepted in the remaining zones since 2012, and one German sticker is valid in every German low emission zone. The same authority notes that many municipalities have removed their zones, so a five year old list can be both unnecessarily alarming and dangerously wrong.

Use the current Umweltbundesamt guidance and zone list. It also states that entering without the required sticker or exemption can bring a EUR 100 fine. Eligibility comes from the emissions data in the papers, not from the motorhome’s colour or the driver’s confidence.

Belgium: one LEZ label, separate city procedures

Brussels requires most foreign registered vehicles to be registered. Its official portal says registration is free and valid for five years. Foreign motorhomes have a specific exemption route: register the vehicle, provide the registration certificate and attach evidence that it is a motorhome, such as a photograph showing the plate. Follow the official Brussels LEZ motorhome exemption procedure.

Antwerp operates its own system. Its official portal says eligible foreign plated vehicles must be registered once, apart from the stated exception for Dutch plates. Check and register through the Antwerp LEZ portal. A Brussels confirmation is not a Belgian season ticket.

Spain: national labels, local restrictions

Spain’s DGT explains that a foreign registered vehicle cannot obtain a Spanish environmental sticker. Vehicles from countries with a recognised environmental classification can use the equivalence table, but access restrictions still come from municipal ordinances. Review the official DGT environmental badge guidance.

If your home country’s badge is not recognised by the city, use that city’s official portal to check foreign plate registration. Barcelona, Madrid and other municipalities do not have to use identical procedures. A navigation app can identify the fastest road. It cannot promise that your plate is authorised to use it.

A 15 minute entry plan

  1. List every city you will enter, including places where the campsite or ring road sits inside a zone.
  2. Open the official zone map and trace the final approach to your overnight stop.
  3. Confirm category, Euro standard, fuel, first registration date and authorised mass.
  4. Identify the required badge, registration, permit or exemption.
  5. Complete it only on an official portal and save confirmation offline.
  6. Add a waypoint before the zone boundary and a park and ride or bypass as plan B.

When the motorhome does not qualify

Do not gamble on an absent camera. Move the campsite outside the zone, use a park and ride, take suburban rail or route around the boundary. Some cities sell day passes or offer exemptions for motorhomes, disabled travellers, historic vehicles or specified journeys. Exemptions are local. Saying another city allowed it is about as useful as showing a train ticket on the metro.

Low emission zone checklist

  • registration certificate and a clear digital copy
  • vehicle category, authorised mass, fuel, Euro standard and first registration date
  • official map for every zone on the route
  • stickers ordered early enough for delivery
  • registrations and exemptions stored offline
  • a legal car park or bypass as backup
  • a final rule check on the day before entry

Frequently asked questions

Does one environmental sticker work across Europe

No. Countries and cities use different stickers, registrations and eligibility criteria. Check every zone individually.

Are motorhomes exempt from low emission zones

Not automatically. Some cities provide a motorhome exemption or special procedure, but it depends on the vehicle category and local rules.

Will navigation always avoid low emission zones

No. Treat avoidance settings as an aid and confirm the official boundary yourself.
