Van Life Budget Calculator: Work Out Your Monthly and First Year Cost Without Instagram Maths

In photographs, van life costs roughly one mug of coffee placed beside an open door. In a spreadsheet it also contains fuel, servicing, insurance, laundry, tolls and the one electrical component that always fails on Friday evening.

There is no honest universal answer to “how much does van life cost?” There is, however, an honest calculation. This guide works out the first year cost of van life, monthly cash flow and the long term economic cost of the vehicle. For a broad European price comparison, also read the real cost of van life in Europe.

One journey has three different costs

The common mistake is to compare rent with fuel alone. A useful van budget has three layers, each answering a different question.

ResultWhat it includesWhat it tells you
Starting cashVehicle, conversion, setup, equipment and reserveWhether you can begin safely
Monthly cash flowBills actually paid during the monthWhether your account survives until payday
Economic costCash flow plus depreciation and annualised costsHow van life really compares with housing

First year cash formula: vehicle purchase plus conversion plus setup costs plus twelve months of spending plus an emergency fund.

True monthly cost formula: current spending plus annual bills divided by twelve plus monthly vehicle depreciation.

Starting cost does not end at the vehicle price

  • purchase price or deposit and financing charges
  • pre purchase inspection, registration, tax and initial insurance
  • repairs, tyres and baseline service needed before travel
  • conversion, electricity, water, heating, ventilation and equipment
  • legal reclassification or weight paperwork where required
  • tools, cables, cookware, bedding and small items that stop being small when added together
  • a reserve for the first serious failure and alternative accommodation

Do not assume every pound or euro spent on a conversion adds the same amount to resale value. A layout perfectly tailored to you may be impressively uninteresting to the next owner. Treat the build as something you use, not a savings account with cupboards.

Fuel calculator: count kilometres, not intentions

The formula is simple: kilometres divided by 100, multiplied by consumption and price per litre. With an illustrative 1200 km, 10.5 litres per 100 km and EUR 1.70 per litre, the result is EUR 214.20. This demonstrates the formula; it is not a fuel price forecast.

Use consumption measured across several fill ups, not the brochure number for an empty van. Add detours, water runs, shopping and the mileage spent finding a stop. The European Commission Weekly Oil Bulletin publishes current fuel price data for EU countries.

Overnight costs: calculate a mix, not a promise

Instead of writing “I will always park for free”, estimate nights in four categories: free legal stop, municipal aire or stellplatz, campsite and emergency accommodation. Multiply nights by realistic regional prices, then add electricity, showers, booking fees and pet charges.

For example, 20 free nights, 6 nights at EUR 18 and 4 nights at EUR 35 total EUR 248 before extras. The same person in peak season may get a very different result. Free parking can still require paid water, service points and responsible waste disposal.

Food: separate normal groceries from travel leakage

You would eat in a house too, so compare two numbers: the full grocery bill and the extra amount created by travel. The second includes restaurants after a long drive, service station purchases, small packs, food waste and tourist town prices.

Batch cooking can save gas and reduce takeaway temptation, but it needs refrigeration and safe storage. Do not buy a large compressor fridge purely to save money. First check whether its energy demand fits your electrical system.

Divide annual bills by twelve

Insurance, vehicle tax, inspection, servicing, tyres, memberships and some subscriptions are not paid monthly, yet they belong in every monthly budget. Add the annual total and divide by twelve. Keep a separate repair reserve based on the age, mileage and parts availability of your vehicle.

Check breakdown cover for motorhome weight and height, towing distance, countries covered and replacement accommodation. The cheapest policy works beautifully until its small print decides your home is too large for the recovery truck.

Internet: EU roaming does not always mean unlimited data

EU roaming generally uses domestic prices during periodic travel, but operators can apply fair use limits. Extended stays abroad or low cost unlimited plans can trigger a data limit or surcharge. The official Your Europe roaming guide explains fair use.

Budget for a primary SIM, backup connection, router where needed and data outside the EU. Do not add satellite internet merely because the dish looks adventurous on a roof. First count how many working days will genuinely lack mobile coverage.

Hidden costs that change the answer

  • Depreciation: the difference between expected resale value and the full cost of purchase and permanent improvements.
  • Cost of capital: money locked in the van cannot work elsewhere, while borrowing creates interest.
  • Alternative accommodation: a garage can keep both your vehicle and your home.
  • Storage and address: belongings outside the van, post and a formal base may cost money.
  • Emergency return: transport home, pet care or a replacement car.
  • Tolls, ferries and zones: pricing by vehicle weight and dimensions can change the route cost.
  • Double expenses: rent, utilities or subscriptions retained during the transition.

  2. Add monthly fixed, variable and reserve amounts.
  3. Create a scenario with fuel 20 percent more expensive.
  4. Add one month of alternative accommodation and one major repair.
  5. Compare the result with current housing, transport and holiday costs.

Reduce spending in the right order

  1. Slow down. Fewer kilometres reduce fuel, tolls and wear at the same time.
  2. Plan the overnight mix. Use free places legally and buy campsite nights for servicing and genuine rest.
  3. Stop leakage. Service stations, impulse purchases, unused subscriptions and missed bookings are easier to cut than heating.
  4. Test before buying. Rent a similar vehicle and spend a wet week in it before ordering an expensive conversion.
  5. Repair only what you can handle safely. A cabin filter is not a braking system or a gas installation.

The 30 day test before changing your life

For one month, save the difference between current spending and your planned van life budget. At the same time, record every cost related to the vehicle, eating out, connectivity and travel. If the simulated budget is already tight, the open road will not magically loosen it. Delay departure, reduce the vehicle cost or increase the reserve.

Frequently asked questions

How much does van life cost per month?

There is no universal figure. Mileage, paid nights, household size, vehicle finance and region dominate the result. Use your own inputs in the formulas above.

How large should a van life emergency fund be?

It should cover a repair, recovery or excess, alternative accommodation and onward travel. Base it on vehicle value, age and parts availability, not a round number copied from somebody else.

Does a solar system always pay for itself?

No. The result depends on electricity use, sunlight, alternative charging and how many paid nights it genuinely prevents.

Is van life cheaper than renting?

It can be, but compare complete costs on both sides, including depreciation, financing, repairs, storage and double expenses during the transition.
