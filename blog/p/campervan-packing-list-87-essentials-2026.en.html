What to Pack for a Campervan Trip and What to Leave Behind: The Complete 87 Item Checklist

A practical campervan packing list with 87 essentials for documents, safety, water, power, cooking, sleep and hygiene, plus the clutter to leave at home.

A first campervan trip has a peculiar talent for making people pack three frying pans and forget the electric hook up adapter. You then carry a cast iron wok across the Alps while charging your phone at reception. A useful campervan packing list does not squeeze your entire home into a vehicle. It prevents specific problems: missing documents, unsafe driving, no water, a cold night and being unable to move after a small failure.

Below are exactly 87 campervan essentials. They are ordered by the consequences of forgetting them, not by the aisle in which a shop sells them. The trip ending items come first. Comfort and campsite items follow.

How to use this packing list

Do not buy every item automatically. Check the equipment already in your vehicle, the number of travellers, weather, overnight style and the countries on your route. A rental may already include much of this kit. Photograph every locker before leaving and record where the valves, fuses and tools live. Darkness is a bad time to meet your own vehicle.

Documents and money: items 1 to 8

  1. Driving licence valid for the vehicle weight and category.
  2. Passport or identity card for every traveller.
  3. Registration document and rental authorisation when relevant.
  4. Insurance certificate with the recovery number.
  5. EHIC or equivalent plus suitable travel insurance.
  6. Vignette and toll confirmations linked to the correct registration.
  7. Two payment methods, preferably from different networks, plus modest cash.
  8. Document copies stored separately and securely on your phone.

Vehicle and safety: items 9 to 20

  1. Warning triangle.
  2. A reflective vest for each person, reachable from the cab.
  3. A properly stocked first aid kit.
  4. A suitable extinguisher secured near the exit.
  5. Smoke alarm.
  6. Carbon monoxide alarm.
  7. Tyre pressure gauge.
  8. 12 V compressor or reliable pump.
  9. Tyre repair kit or spare wheel with the correct tools.
  10. Wheel wrench and jack rated for the motorhome.
  11. Head torch, because two free hands beat a phone light.
  12. Basic tool kit with screwdrivers, pliers, spanners, fuses and repair tape.

Check mandatory equipment for every country. Rules for vests, lights, tyres and projecting loads differ across Europe. Use official information from Your Europe before crossing borders.

Water, electricity and gas: items 21 to 32

  1. Food grade fresh water hose.
  2. A set of tap connectors.
  3. Collapsible backup water container.
  4. Separate drinking water for the first day.
  5. Gloves reserved for toilet and waste water servicing.
  6. Correctly rated CEE campsite cable.
  7. CEE adapter for the local connection.
  8. Socket tester or protection recommended for your installation.
  9. Extension cable that is disconnected and cooled before coiling.
  10. USB charger with enough ports.
  11. Power bank.
  12. Correct gas bottle or filling adapter researched before departure.

Never mix the drinking water hose with sanitation equipment. That sounds obvious until two identical coiled hoses share a locker. Colour code them.

Sleeping and clothing: items 33 to 45

  1. Fitted sheet for the unusually shaped mattress.
  2. Duvet or sleeping bag suited to the coldest night.
  3. Pillow that still fits when the bed is stowed.
  4. Warm layer for sleeping.
  5. Waterproof jacket.
  6. One fleece or warm sweatshirt.
  7. Clothes for three to five days, even on a longer journey.
  8. Quick drying underwear.
  9. Walking shoes.
  10. Light shower sandals.
  11. Sun hat.
  12. Warm hat for cold stops.
  13. Closable laundry bag.

Pack outfits, not isolated garments. Trousers that work with only one shirt have hired a private locker. On most trips, a few days of clothing and one laundry stop beat a wardrobe on wheels.

Kitchen and food: items 46 to 60

  1. One good saucepan with a lid.
  2. One frying pan.
  3. Sharp knife with a safe cover.
  4. Small chopping board.
  5. Spatula or wooden spoon.
  6. One plate, mug and cutlery set per person.
  7. Leakproof food containers.
  8. Dish cloth and small tea towel.
  9. Dish soap used only where waste water can be collected.
  10. Matches or lighter stored away from the hob.
  11. Tin opener and corkscrew if you genuinely use it.
  12. Salt, pepper, oil and four favourite spices instead of an entire drawer.
  13. Two complete meals requiring no cooking.
  14. Shelf stable food for the first day.
  15. Waste bags in two sizes.

If you have no active refrigeration, use the practical guide to campervan cooking without a fridge.

Hygiene and toilet: items 61 to 70

  1. Quick drying towel.
  2. Wash bag with small containers.
  3. Toilet paper suitable for your system.
  4. Toilet treatment recommended by the manufacturer.
  5. Hand soap.
  6. Hand sanitiser.
  7. Wipes used sparingly and never dropped into a cassette toilet.
  8. Small dustpan and brush.
  9. Quick surface cleaner.
  10. Line and pegs for drying towels.

Our campervan shower and toilet guide explains water use, emptying and realistic hygiene routines.

Parking and outdoor living: items 71 to 79

  1. Levelling ramps.
  2. Small spirit level.
  3. Two lightweight chairs.
  4. Table only if the conversion does not provide one.
  5. Easy clean mat for the doorway.
  6. Security cable for bikes or equipment.
  7. Insect repellent.
  8. Sun cream.
  9. Small umbrella or tarp that can be used within site rules.

Chairs and an awning turn parking into camping in the eyes of many authorities. Do not deploy them where only parking is permitted. Read where to sleep in a van legally in Europe before choosing a spot.

Technology and the backup plan: items 80 to 87

  1. Phone with offline maps downloaded.
  2. Secure mount that does not block the driver's view.
  3. Spare cable for the main phone.
  4. Emergency and recovery numbers saved on paper too.
  5. Weather forecast for the route, not only the destination.
  6. Two backup overnight stops saved before arrival.
  7. Vehicle height, width, length and weight written in the cab.
  8. A departure checklist saved in the Travel Rules app.

What to leave at home

  • more than one set of pans
  • sports gear packed purely in case a new personality appears
  • full size cosmetic bottles
  • glassware without a compelling reason
  • clothes needing special laundry
  • a generator when you have not researched noise, exhaust and fuel rules
  • gadgets with no secured place while driving
  • more food than you can store safely

Every object must answer three questions: will I use it this week, where will it live while driving and what happens under heavy braking? If the answers are maybe, somewhere and it flies, the object has removed itself from the list.

Pack by payload, not empty cupboards

An empty locker does not create legal payload. Check maximum authorised mass, actual weight with fuel, water, people and equipment, plus axle limits. Keep heavy items low and close to the axles. Weigh the loaded vehicle before a long journey.

Frequently asked questions

What matters most on a first campervan trip?

Documents, safety equipment, water, hook up cable, warm bedding, a functioning toilet and two possible overnight stops. Decorations can wait.

How many clothes do I need for a week?

Three to five days of layers plus one laundry stop usually works. Pack garments that combine with one another.

Do I need all 87 items?

No. Treat this as a risk check. Remove equipment already supplied or irrelevant to your route, but make the decision deliberately.
